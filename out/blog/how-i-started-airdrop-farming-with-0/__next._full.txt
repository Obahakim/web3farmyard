<p>There was a time when I was completely broke, but still hungry for opportunity.<br>No capital, No fancy setup, No deep connections. Just curiosity, internet access when I could get it, and the feeling that there had to be a way forward.<br>That was when I discovered something that changed my mindset:</p>



<p>You can start positioning for airdrops with $0.</p>



<h2>How It Started</h2>



<p>A lot of people think you need money to enter crypto. Sometimes that is true for trading or investing. But airdrop farming introduced me to a different path, one where your effort, consistency, time, and curiosity could become capital.<br>So I decided to try.<br>At the time, I did not even have my own computer available, so I borrowed a friend’s PC and locked in.<br>This was during the era when SocialFi projects were hot. New platforms were experimenting with the idea of rewarding users for engagement, participation, and attention. One project I joined was a Web3 advertising platform that promised rewards for watching ads.<br>And honestly, it made sense.</p>



<p>People had been watching ads for free for years. Suddenly, someone was saying: What if users got paid for their attention That idea caught my interest immediately. I joined, participated, and eventually made around $40. My friend made a bit more.</p>



<p>Was it life-changing money? No.</p>



<p>But it was honest money. More importantly, it proved something to me: Even with zero capital, the internet still had doors that could open.That small reward was the spark.</p>



<h3>The Mindset Shift That Changed Everything</h3>



<p>Once I saw it was possible to earn from participating early in Web3, I became obsessed with learning more.</p>



<p>I asked myself:</p>



<p>What other opportunities exist? <br>How do people qualify for airdrops?<br>What separates those who earn from those who complain?<br>Can someone broke actually build something from nothing?</p>



<p>That curiosity became fuel.</p>



<p>And if I’m being honest, being broke can sometimes create a powerful hunger. You start paying attention differently. You start valuing information differently.</p>



<p>When you have no money, knowledge becomes your first currency.</p>



<h2>My First Real Strategy: Learn Everything</h2>



<p>I started reading guides on X (Twitter), joining discussions, following accounts that talked about airdrops, testnets, DeFi, wallets, and new projects,joined forums, followed builders, read threads.</p>



<p>I watched people explain farming strategies.</p>



<p>At first, I did not understand everything. A lot of crypto language sounds like aliens arguing in code.</p>



<p>But I kept consuming information.</p>



<p>Soon, the algorithm began feeding me better content because I had trained it with my interests.That is something beginners underestimate. Your Feed Can Become Your Teacher. If you follow nonsense, you get nonsense. If you follow builders, researchers, and smart farmers, your timeline slowly becomes a classroom.</p>



<p>That is why I tell beginners: The first investment you make in Web3 should be attention. Not money. Attention.</p>



<h3>Why Starting With $0 Was Actually an Advantage</h3>



<p>This may sound strange, but starting broke gave me an edge.</p>



<p>Why?<br>Because I had nothing to lose.I was not worried about portfolio drawdowns. I was not emotionally attached to token prices.I was not risking thousands, I was simply learning, experimenting, and positioning.<br>I could create wallets, test platforms, claim faucet tokens, try new products, and observe how ecosystems worked.</p>



<p>Now, that does not mean you should be careless.<br>Even if your wallet has $0 today, it may become eligible for future rewards later. If it gets hacked because you clicked phishing links or signed bad approvals, you may lose future upside.</p>



<p>So yes, start broke if that is your reality, but still move smart.</p>



<p>Protect wallets.Double-check links.Use burner wallets when needed; Security matters before money arrives, not after.</p>



<h2>What I Actually Did Every Day</h2>



<p>Many people imagine airdrop farming as some magical hack.</p>



<p>Reality is more boring.</p>



<p>It looked like this:</p>



<p>•   Reading project updates
•   Creating wallets
•   Joining waitlists
•   Testing dApps
•   Claiming faucet tokens
•   Doing simple tasks
•   Following announcements
•   Learning ecosystems
•   Staying consistent</p>



<p>That consistency compounds quietly. One day of effort feels small. Three months of effort becomes positioning. </p>



<p>Platforms That Helped Me Early</p>



<p>As I kept learning, I found platforms like <a href="https://app.galxe.com/">Galxe</a> , <a href="https://zealy.io/">Zealy</a> and <a href="https://taskon.xyz">TaskOn</a></p>



<p>These sites often hosted campaigns where users could complete tasks such as:</p>



<p>•   Following social accounts
•   Joining communities
•   Testing products
•   Using wallets
•   Completing on-chain actions
•   Participating in ecosystem quests</p>



<p>Sometimes the rewards were points, sometimes NFTs, tokens and other times small stablecoin rewards.</p>



<p>I remember periods where TaskOn tasks earned me $5 here, $10 there, plus token rewards.To some people, that sounds tiny. But when you are building from zero, tiny wins matter. Tiny wins create momentum.bTiny wins prove effort can translate into value.</p>



<h2>The Mistake Beginners Make</h2>



<p>At one point, I was joining too many things. Every new project looked exciting, every thread said “next big airdrop.” Every community promised something huge.<br>That leads to chaos. You start ten farms. Forget six. Half-complete three. Burn out on one. And then feel like nothing works.<br>My Advice: Focus Beats Frenzy; Choose a manageable number of quality opportunities.</p>



<p>For example:</p>



<p>•   3 testnets
•   3 active ecosystems
•   2 free social reward platforms
•   1 long-term conviction project</p>



<p>That is far better than trying to chase fifty random projects. Crypto punishes scattered energy.</p>



<h3>The Biggest Lesson: Projects Want Real Users</h3>



<p>This changed how I farmed forever. At first, I thought testnet transactions alone were enough. Spam swaps. Bridge tokens. Increase volume. Repeat.<br>But over time, projects became smarter.<br>They realized many people only wanted free tokens and had no interest in the product itself. So reward systems evolved.<br>Projects began looking for:</p>



<p>•   Real usage
•   Genuine feedback
•   Community participation
•   Consistency
•   Helpful members
•   Early believers
•   Product testers</p>



<p>That is when I understood something important:<br>The best airdrop farmers are not extractors. They are contributors.<br>I started joining Discord servers and actually participating. Not spamming “gm.” Not begging for tokens. Real participation. I asked questions. Shared feedback. Joined discussions. Observed how teams communicated. Watched how moderators handled issues. You can learn a lot from community behavior. Some teams are disciplined and transparent, some are disorganized. Some vanish during pressure, some treat users badly. I once left a project because the team’s behavior in community spaces revealed poor leadership. Eventually, the project collapsed.<br>That experience taught me this: Community is due diligence. Watch how teams behave when nobody is forcing them to behave well.</p>



<p>Another Lesson I Learnt: You Don’t Need to Fake a Personality<br>Another lesson I learned was not to force an identity. Some people thrive making educational threads, others are good at memes.
