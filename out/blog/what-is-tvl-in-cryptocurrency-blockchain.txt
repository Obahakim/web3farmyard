									<h2 style="--tw-border-spacing-x: 0; --tw-border-spacing-y: 0; --tw-translate-x: 0; --tw-translate-y: 0; --tw-rotate: 0; --tw-skew-x: 0; --tw-skew-y: 0; --tw-scale-x: 1; --tw-scale-y: 1; --tw-scroll-snap-strictness: proximity; --tw-ring-offset-width: 0px; --tw-ring-offset-color: #fff; --tw-ring-color: rgba(59,130,246,.5); --tw-ring-offset-shadow: 0 0 #0000; --tw-ring-shadow: 0 0 #0000; --tw-shadow: 0 0 #0000; --tw-shadow-colored: 0 0 #0000; color: #111111; font-size: 47.1968px; max-width: 645px; font-family: Manrope, sans-serif; white-space: pre-wrap; min-width: 1px; margin: revert auto !important revert auto !important;"> </h2>
<p><span style="font-size: 20.7649px;">If you’ve hung around crypto folks for even a short while, you’ve probably heard phrases like:</span></p>
<p>“This protocol has massive TVL.”<br />“TVL is growing fast.”<br />“Low TVL gem.”</p>
<p><span style="text-wrap-mode: initial; letter-spacing: -0.1px;">Let’s break it down in plain language.</span></p>
<p><!-- /wp:paragraph --><!-- wp:paragraph --></p>
<h2>What Does TVL Mean?</h2>
<p><!-- /wp:paragraph --><!-- wp:paragraph --></p>
<p>TVL stands for Total Value Locked.<br />It’s simply the total amount of money sitting inside a crypto protocol, app, or blockchain ecosystem.</p>
<p><!-- /wp:paragraph --><!-- wp:paragraph --></p>
<p>That money could be:</p>
<p><!-- /wp:paragraph --><!-- wp:list --></p>
<ul>
<li style="list-style-type: none;">
<ul><!-- wp:list-item --></ul>
</li>
</ul>
<ul>
<li style="list-style-type: none;">
<ul>
<li>Stablecoins like USDC or USDT</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>
<p><!-- /wp:list-item --><!-- wp:list-item --></p>
<ul>
<li style="list-style-type: none;">
<ul>
<li>Ethereum or Bitcoin wrappers</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>
<p><!-- /wp:list-item --><!-- wp:list-item --></p>
<ul>
<li style="list-style-type: none;">
<ul>
<li>Other tokens</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>
<p><!-- /wp:list-item --><!-- wp:list-item --></p>
<ul>
<li style="list-style-type: none;">
<ul>
<li>Liquidity pool assets</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>
<p><!-- /wp:list-item --><!-- wp:list-item --></p>
<ul>
<li style="list-style-type: none;">
<ul>
<li>Staked funds</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>
<p><!-- /wp:list-item --><!-- wp:list-item --></p>
<ul>
<li style="list-style-type: none;">
<ul>
<li>Collateral for borrowing</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>
<p><!-- /wp:list-item --></p>
<p><!-- /wp:list --><!-- wp:paragraph --></p>
<p>Think of TVL as the amount of capital currently “inside the machine.”<br />If a DeFi platform has $500 million deposited, then its TVL is $500 million. Simple.</p>
<p><!-- /wp:paragraph --><!-- wp:paragraph --></p>
<p>Why Is It Called “Locked”? You may ask</p>
<p><!-- /wp:paragraph --><!-- wp:paragraph --></p>
<p>The word “locked” might confuse many beginners. It doesn’t mean your money is trapped forever.<br />It means your funds are committed inside smart contracts rather than just sitting idle in your wallet.</p>
<p><!-- /wp:paragraph --><!-- wp:paragraph --></p>
<p>For example:</p>
<p><!-- /wp:paragraph --><!-- wp:list --></p>
<ul>
<li style="list-style-type: none;">
<ul><!-- wp:list-item --></ul>
</li>
</ul>
<ul>
<li style="list-style-type: none;">
<ul>
<li>You deposit tokens into a lending app</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>
<p><!-- /wp:list-item --><!-- wp:list-item --></p>
<ul>
<li style="list-style-type: none;">
<ul>
<li>You provide liquidity to a DEX</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>
<p><!-- /wp:list-item --><!-- wp:list-item --></p>
<ul>
<li style="list-style-type: none;">
<ul>
<li>You stake tokens for rewards</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>
<p><!-- /wp:list-item --></p>
<p><!-- /wp:list --><!-- wp:paragraph --></p>
<p>Those assets are now working inside the system. That’s what “locked” means. LOCKED INSIDE THE SYSTEM until you choose to take it out.</p>
<p><!-- /wp:paragraph --><!-- wp:paragraph --></p>
<p>Let’s use Traditional financial institutions for reference;</p>
<p><!-- /wp:paragraph --><!-- wp:paragraph --></p>
<p>Imagine a bank vault. The more money people trust the bank with, the more money sits in the vault.</p>
<p><!-- /wp:paragraph --><!-- wp:paragraph --></p>
<p>Now replace the bank with blockchain code.<br />That vault is a smart contract.<br />The money inside it is TVL.</p>
<p><!-- /wp:paragraph --><!-- wp:paragraph --></p>
<p>Higher TVL often means more people trust the platform enough to put their money there.</p>
<p><!-- /wp:paragraph --><!-- wp:paragraph --></p>
<p>Why TVL Matters in Crypto</p>
<p><!-- /wp:paragraph --><!-- wp:paragraph --></p>
<p>How do people judge if a protocol is important?<br />One swift answer is: How much capital is using it?</p>
<p><!-- /wp:paragraph --><!-- wp:paragraph --></p>
<p>TVL helps measure:</p>
<p><span style="letter-spacing: -0.1px; text-wrap-mode: initial;">             1. Trust – More deposits “can” signal confidence</span></p>
<p><span style="letter-spacing: -0.1px;">              2. Adoption – More money often means more users</span></p>
<p><span style="letter-spacing: -0.1px;">              3. Liquidity – For exchanges, deeper liquidity means                                  smoother trading.</span></p>
<p><span style="letter-spacing: -0.1px;">            4. Growth – Rising TVL can show expansion.</span></p>
<p><!-- /wp:list-item --></p>
<p><!-- /wp:list --><!-- wp:paragraph --></p>
<p>Example:</p>
<p><!-- /wp:paragraph --><!-- wp:paragraph --></p>
<p>Let’s say a lending platform has:</p>
<p><!-- /wp:paragraph --><!-- wp:list --></p>
<ul>
<li style="list-style-type: none;">
<ul><!-- wp:list-item --></ul>
</li>
</ul>
<ul>
<li style="list-style-type: none;">
<ul>
<li>$40 million in ETH</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>
<p><!-- /wp:list-item --><!-- wp:list-item --></p>
<ul>
<li style="list-style-type: none;">
<ul>
<li>$25 million in USDC</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>
<p><!-- /wp:list-item --><!-- wp:list-item --></p>
<ul>
<li style="list-style-type: none;">
<ul>
<li>$10 million in BTC wrappers</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>
<p><!-- /wp:list-item --></p>
<p><!-- /wp:list --><!-- wp:paragraph --></p>
<p>Total = $75 million TVL.<br />That means users have placed $75 million worth of assets into the protocol.</p>
<p><!-- /wp:paragraph --><!-- wp:paragraph --></p>
<h2>Does Higher TVL Always Mean it’s a Better protocol?</h2>
<p>No. TVL is useful, but not magic. A project can have high TVL because:</p>
<p><!-- /wp:paragraph --><!-- wp:list --></p>
<ul>
<li style="list-style-type: none;">
<ul><!-- wp:list-item --></ul>
</li>
</ul>
<ul>
<li style="list-style-type: none;">
<ul>
<li>Incentives are paying users to deposit temporarily</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>
<p><!-- /wp:list-item --><!-- wp:list-item --></p>
<ul>
<li style="list-style-type: none;">
<ul>
<li>Whales parked money there</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>
<p><!-- /wp:list-item --><!-- wp:list-item --></p>
<ul>
<li style="list-style-type: none;">
<ul>
<li>Token prices pumped</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>
<p><!-- /wp:list-item --><!-- wp:list-item --></p>
<ul>
<li style="list-style-type: none;">
<ul>
<li>Users are chasing rewards, not loyal</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>
<p><!-- /wp:list-item --></p>
<p><!-- /wp:list --><!-- wp:paragraph --></p>
<p>So high TVL doesn’t automatically mean safe or sustainable. Remember Mufasa, A big house can still have termites.<br /><img decoding="async" role="img" draggable="false" src="https://s.w.org/images/core/emoji/17.0.2/svg/1f9d8.svg" alt="🧘" /></p>
<p><!-- /wp:paragraph --><!-- wp:paragraph --></p>
<p>Low TVL Can Also Be Interesting</p>
<p><!-- /wp:paragraph --><!-- wp:paragraph --></p>
<p>Some investors look for “low TVL gems” — projects with small TVL but strong products.<br />If adoption grows later, TVL can rise sharply.</p>
<p><!-- /wp:paragraph --><!-- wp:paragraph --></p>
<p>But beware: many low TVL projects are low for a reason; nobody wants them. So caution matters.</p>
<p><!-- /wp:paragraph --><!-- wp:paragraph --></p>
<p>TVL vs Market Cap</p>
<p><!-- /wp:paragraph --><!-- wp:paragraph --></p>
<p>Beginners often confuse these two:</p>
<p><span style="letter-spacing: -0.1px;">                 Market Cap = token price × supply</span></p>
<p><span style="letter-spacing: -0.1px;">                 TVL = actual assets deposited in the protocol</span></p>
<p><!-- /wp:list-item --></p>
<p><!-- /wp:list --><!-- wp:paragraph --></p>
<p>Example:</p>
<p><span style="letter-spacing: -0.1px;">                Token market cap = $2 billion</span></p>
<p><span style="letter-spacing: -0.1px;">                TVL = $150 million</span></p>
<p><!-- /wp:list-item --></p>
<p><!-- /wp:list --><!-- wp:paragraph --></p>
<p>That suggests the token valuation may be high compared to real usage.</p>
<p><!-- /wp:paragraph --><!-- wp:paragraph --></p>
<h2>Can TVL Go Up Without New Users?</h2>
<p><!-- /wp:paragraph --><!-- wp:paragraph --></p>
<p>Yes. If ETH for instance doubles in price, the dollar value of deposited ETH also doubles.<br />Same token, bigger number.</p>
<p><!-- /wp:paragraph --><!-- wp:paragraph --></p>
<p>So always ask: Did TVL grow because of new users, or because token prices went up? Huge difference.</p>
